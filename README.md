# 👥 HR Attrition Analysis Dashboard

An interactive Excel dashboard built with Claude to analyze employee attrition patterns across departments, roles, and demographics for a 1,470-employee workforce.

---

## 🛠️ Tools & Techniques Used

- **Microsoft Excel**
- **Built with Claude** — dashboard structure, KPI formulas, and filters generated with AI assistance (no Python/coding involved)
- **Dynamic Filters** — Department, Overtime, Gender slicers driving every KPI and chart
- **Formulas** — `AVERAGEIFS`, `COUNTIFS`, % attrition rate calculations
- **KPI Cards** — Employees in view, Attrition Rate, Avg. Monthly Income, Avg. Tenure, Avg. Job Satisfaction

---

## 📈 Key Insights

- **Overall attrition rate: 16.1%** (238 of 1,470 employees), with an average tenure of 7.0 years and job satisfaction of 2.73/4
- **Overtime is the strongest attrition driver** — employees working overtime leave at **30.5%** vs. just **10.4%** for those who don't, nearly a **3x** gap, and this holds across every department
- **Sales Representatives churn the most** by role, at **39.8%** — more than double the company average — while Managers (4.9%) and Research Directors (2.5%) are the most stable
- **Frequent travelers leave 3x more often** (24.9%) than employees with no travel requirement (8.0%)
- **Single employees churn at 25.5%**, roughly double the rate of married (12.5%) or divorced (10.1%) employees
- **No stock options = 24.4% attrition**, dropping to under 10% for any employee holding equity — the clearest retention lever in the data
- **Leavers are younger, earn less, and stay shorter** — avg. age 33.6 vs. 37.6, avg. income $4,787 vs. $6,833, avg. tenure 5.1 vs. 7.4 years for those who stayed
- **Salary hike % and training frequency show almost no link to attrition** — not worth over-investing in as standalone retention fixes
